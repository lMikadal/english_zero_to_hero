# Findings (known, accepted, for later)

ปัญหาที่รู้แล้วและยอมรับไว้ชั่วคราว ทบทวนเป็นรอบเพื่อหารูปแบบ (ไม่ไล่แก้ทีละจุด)

## การเทียบคำตอบ (grading) และกฎ answer-forms

- **Under-acceptance ที่ยอมรับ:** `'s` พับเป็น `is` เฉพาะเมื่อคำถัดไปไม่ใช่กริยาแน่ๆ (determiner, เลข, คำบุพบท/สถานที่ในรายการปิด, `-ing`, adjective ที่ไม่ใช่ participle) คำอื่นทั้งหมด (adverb, สรรพนาม, คำที่ไม่รู้จัก) ไม่พับ ผู้เรียนที่พิมพ์รูปอีกแบบจะถูกตัดว่าผิด และกฎ `answer-forms` บังคับให้ผู้เขียนใส่ทั้งสองรูป จึงไม่มี over-acceptance โดยโครงสร้าง
- **คำนามทั่วไป + `'s`** (`My mother's a teacher` กับ `My mother is a teacher`) ไม่พับ กฎ `answer-forms` ตรวจเฉพาะกรณีที่ตามด้วย a/an/the/not/คำบุพบทสถานที่ (บริบทที่เป็น possessive ไม่ได้) และบังคับให้ใส่ `X is ...` คำนามทั่วไป + `'s` + บริบทอื่น (`my mother's happy`) ไม่ถูกตรวจ เพราะแยก possessive ไม่ได้แน่
- **`one` กับเลข 1:** สรรพนาม `one` พับกับตัวเลข ทำให้ `the red 1` / `no 1 is here` ถูกรับเมื่อเฉลยคือ `the red one` / `no one is here` เป็นการรับเกิน ที่ยอมรับเพราะพบน้อยมากและแก้ได้ด้วย `strict: true`
- **`'d`** ที่ไม่ใช่ก่อน like/love ไม่พับ (would หรือ had?) ผู้เขียนต้องใส่รูปเต็ม
- dictation รับตัวเลขแทนคำอ่านเลข (`10` แทน `ten`) ซึ่งเป็นการตัดสินใจด้านการสอน รอผู้ใช้ตัดสิน

## Round 4

- **Over-acceptance the next-word test cannot prevent:** possession written as `'s` without `got` (British: `He's no idea`, `He's two cars`, `She's long hair`). After `no`, a number or `many`, `'s` folds to `is`, so the wrong `He is two cars` is accepted. Mitigation: the validator gives a WARNING (not an error) when `he's`/`she's` is followed by no / many / a lot of / a number 2-10 plus a bank noun that is not a unit of time. Authors write possession as `has` / `has got`.
- `He has not finished` is rejected for an accepted `He's not finished` ('s before "not" + participle is not folded); the author lists both forms and the rule asks for it.
- Added to the safe list: weather adjectives, days and months (not `may`/`march`), nationalities/languages, and object/possessive pronouns only after he/she/it. Not added: `half`, pronouns after a wh-word.

## Round 5 (validator rules from the week-3 review)

Rules added: `arrange-question`, `sound-words`, `phoneme-tags`, `cast-consistency` (warning), `grammar-not-yet`; `very` folds in `'s` grading. What they cannot see:

- **arrange-question** skips negatives (`I am not ...`), wh-questions, `Yes, ...`, sentences with a comma, two clauses, `Here is ...`, contractions (`She's ...`) and `do/does` statements. It also skips a statement with `and/but/or` after the verb (`He is a teacher and a doctor`), so some valid questions are not demanded (false negatives). `Is my name Sam` is odd but correct English and was added on day 1 (use `statementOnly` if an author disagrees).
- **sound-words** only reads `/x/ ใน w1, w2 และ w3` / `/x/ in w1, w2 and w3`. Other wordings ("เสียง v ใน five") and section titles/objectives are not read. Words with no entry (and inflected forms such as `women`, `them`) are a warning only. A name list inside quotes works, a list after `ไม่ใช่`/`ต่างจาก`/`not` is skipped.
- **phoneme-tags** cannot know that a minimal pair contrasts two sounds when the intro names one sound and no item is tagged with two codes yet. Idea: a `phonemes` field on `phoneme_intro` for the contrast set, or derive the sounds of a pair from IPA.
- **cast-consistency** reads `Name is N`, `I am Name ... I am N` (one speaker only) and `He/She is N` after a one-name sentence. Cannot detect: ages in other wording, `How old is Ann?` + a far answer, `They are ten`, speaker-labelled dialogues (`Ann: I am ten`), names missing from `PROPER_NAMES`, and different weeks. It also treats two different people sharing a name as one (it is a warning for that reason). Week 2 had Ann at 8/9/10/15/20 and Ben at 7/9/10/13/30; fixed (children renamed Nok and Jim, ages made consistent).
- **grammar-not-yet**: the table has `has` (day 29), `does`, `goes`, regular third-person -s of every bank verb, `but` (all day 36). **Open decision for the curriculum owner:** `docs/CURRICULUM.md` gives no day for `but` (it is in `FUNCTION_WORDS`, so taught-words allows it everywhere); 36 is a placeholder and the entry is a warning only. Not yet in the table (add when a lesson needs it): `-ing` continuous contrast, past forms (`was/were`), `can`, comparatives, plural `-s` of nouns is fine. Forms inside a bilingual question text (`What does Ann say?`) and sound drills are not checked.
- **'very' in `'s` folding:** `really`, `so`, `too`, `quite` and `very much` are deliberately NOT transparent (`He's really done it`, `He's very much done it` are perfects). `He's very tired` stays unfolded like `He's not tired`: authors list both forms.
- **Ideas, not done:** check minimal pairs against a pronunciation dictionary (do the two words really differ by the named phonemes?); warn when a vocab word used in a sound claim has no entry (add `them`, `women` to the bank).

## Round 5, review follow-ups

- **`very` + a possessive 's:** `He's very little time` is folded to `He is very little time` (`little` is in the adjective list), the same already-known British-has case as `He's two cars`; the answer-forms warning for possession written as `'s` does not look through `very`.
- **grammar-not-yet** generates third-person -s forms only from bank verbs that have NO noun entry and a word that is not itself a bank word or an interjection. So `watches`, `plays`, `likes`, `costs` are not caught when the verb also has a noun entry (`watch`, `play`) or has no entry yet; add them to `GRAMMAR_NOT_BEFORE` by hand when a lesson needs it. `thanks` is never a generated form. `do not` / `don't` is gated at day 36 (Present Simple, `docs/CURRICULUM.md` week 6, which does not name negatives separately); `Do you ...?` questions stay allowed.
- **phoneme-tags** would see two sounds if a `phoneme_intro.ipa` ever held a transcription such as `"/θ/ as in /θriː/"` (it counts every `/.../` symbol); keep `ipa` to the sounds introduced.
- **arrange-question** builds a question only from a positive subject definition (pronoun, determiner, name, bank noun/adjective, number, `and`, place phrase). A subject with a word the bank does not know (an unknown noun, `parents` when only `parent` exists is fine) gets no demand, so a missing bank entry makes the rule silent, not wrong.
- **sound-words** normalises ASCII g, ʧ, ʤ, ɹ and lets `/ɑ/ /ɔ/ /ɜ/` match the long vowel of the bank; `/i/` and `/u/` are NOT matched with `iː` / `uː` (different sounds).
- **cast-consistency** strips `Name:` labels and attributes a labelled line's `I am N` to the speaker. Found Tom (10 vs 40) and Ann (30 vs 20) in week 2; the day-11 boy is now Max and Ann is twenty on day 11.

## Round 6 (validator rules from the week-4 review)

- **UK/US spelling folding** (`src/lib/answers/spelling.ts`, closed table, UK is the canonical form). Not folded, on purpose: anything not in the table (a US spelling nobody listed is simply wrong until it is added), possessive or hyphenated tokens (`colour's`, `colour-blind`), and pairs where the other spelling is another word (`check`/`cheque`, `story`/`storey`, `whisky`/`whiskey`, `spelt`, `smelt`, `kerb`/`curb`). Known over-acceptance: `practise` (verb) and `practice` (noun) are one word for grading, so a learner who writes the noun with `-ise` is not corrected; `tyre`/`tire` and `meter`/`metre` likewise fold the other meaning of the US word. The arrange_words tiles show the author's spelling, only the typed answer is folded. There is no rule that flags a now-redundant `accept` (nothing flags redundant contraction variants either), so the day-26 `gray` accept was removed by hand; a redundant accept is harmless.
- **vocab-spelling** only checks single-word headwords (a multi-word headword such as `favourite colour` is skipped) and only words of the table; the other spelling lives in `forms.usSpelling` / `forms.ukSpelling`, which counts as a surface form of the word (`taught-words` allows `gray` once `grey` is taught). The UI does not render `forms` anywhere, so it is unaffected.
- **thai-names** is conservative by design (Thai has no spaces): it only sees a near-miss standing between a listed particle (ของ/กับ/และ ... and อายุ/อยู่/เป็น/สี ...) or the string ends, never in `tipTh`. It does not see a misspelling next to another word, or the wrong person's name. `Mai`, `Nok`, `Lek`, `Fon` have Thai spellings that are everyday words (ไหม, นก, เล็ก, ฝน), so their misspellings are not checked at all. Names without a Thai spelling give a warning; `Lisa` (ลิซ่า), `Jen`, `Fon` have a spelling chosen here because the lessons never write them in Thai yet: change it freely before the first use. No content disagreed in weeks 1-4 (every name was already spelled one way), the two incidents were fixed in PR #40.
- **cast-props** reads only the patterns listed in `docs/CONTENT_SCHEMA.md`. It cannot see `I have an umbrella. It is orange.` (the incident of day 23 was of this kind and is invisible to it), `Her bag is brown`, or plural things. A new bag bought by the character (day 26) is a legitimate change and would be a warning to read, not to fix.
- **Multiple-choice sanity** (identical choices, `answer` outside `choices`, identical minimal-pair words, blanks vs answers) was already enforced as errors by `src/lib/content/validate.ts`; nothing was added.

## Round 6, review follow-ups

- **Sense-dependent spelling pairs still fold in grading.** The US spelling of `practice`/`practise`, `tire`/`tyre`, `license`/`licence`, `program`/`programme`, `meter`/`metre` is also a correct UK word of another sense, so grading accepts `I tyre easily` for `I tire easily`, `the gas metre`, `a computer programme`, and `analyses`/`analyzes` fold too. Accepted risk at A1 (next to the practise note above). `vocab-spelling` makes no demand for a headword on the sense-dependent US side (`isSenseDependent` in `spelling.ts`); the UK side (`tyre`, `licence`, `programme`, `metre`, `practise`) still demands the US spelling.
- **thai-names is a warning with a known-word filter.** A 3-4 letter Thai name is within two edits of dozens of everyday words, so the rule reports only at one edit (two for 5+ letters, vowel signs and tone marks not counted), never a word of `COMMON_THAI_WORDS` or of the Thai glosses of the vocab bank, and only a warning. Measured recall on the real lessons (every exact name spelling spoiled three ways): 183/306 = 0.598; the misses stand next to a particle outside BEFORE/AFTER. A misspelling that is itself an everyday word is not found.
- **cast-props** stops a `has` list at the next owner, and does not compare an owner's noun when one sentence gives two colours for it (`a brown dog and a white dog`); two things of one noun spread over two sentences are still compared and can warn wrongly.

## Round 8 (validator rules from the week-7 reviews)

- **What fired on the 49 lessons and what changed:** `fronted-variant` 1 real gap (d048-write-03 lacked "Usually Nan takes a train to school": added); `minimal-pair-accents` found `foot`-`food` on day 9 (two sounds: the vowel AND the final consonant; the third pair was removed, two true pairs remain) and 3 false positives from the tokeniser (US r-coloured vowel in work-walk, bird-bed), fixed in the rule; `grammar-note-length` found no length problem, and 2 false positives for compact "A ✗ / B ✗ → A2 ✓ / B2 ✓" lists (fixed in the rule); `cast-facts` found a real contradiction (the vocab example "Leo sometimes listens to music" against d044-read-01 "He often listens to music"; the example now says "Leo sometimes watches TV", the same as the reading and d044-listen-03). `word-blocklist`, `stress-claims`: nothing.
- **fronted-variant** known false positive: a trailing `after X` can modify the object ("I like the class after lunch"); fronting then changes the meaning. A known false negative: a frontable phrase in the middle of the sentence, an `in/on/at` place phrase ("in the park"), and any item that accepts one fronted form but not the other with a different subject. It only reads `arrange_words` and `translate_th_en`.
- **minimal-pair-accents** is as good as the IPA of the bank and only sees pairs where both words are entries (most drill words are not); a pair of two different words that happen to share IPA gives "0 sounds". Stress marks and `(r)` are ignored; `ɜːr`, `ɑːr` count as one sound but `ˈveri` (r before a vowel) does not merge.
- **stress-claims** reads capitals inside a Latin word. Syllable counting is by vowel groups (a silent final e of the prefix is not counted); a word with an irregular spelling can be mis-numbered (warning only). Only words with an entry are checked, and a word with no primary stress mark (one syllable) is skipped.
- **grammar-note-length** counts code points minus Thai vowel signs and tone marks; the header row counts toward the 6 rows; it does not check the number of columns (the template says 2-3).
- **cast-facts** limits are in `docs/CONTENT_SCHEMA.md`. It does not read the cast of grammar cards; it only reads lessons (not checkpoints).
- **Recommendation for a persistent `content/cast.json` (name -> facts): not yet.** Reasons: (1) the in-week machinery already catches the contradictions that were found (ages, colours, frequencies); the cross-week ones would need the same patterns plus a normal form for verbs/objects, which the in-week rule only gets away with because the sentences are close in time and wording; (2) a hand-kept file is a second source of truth: every new fact (a pet, a job, a city) must be written there AND in a lesson, and the validator can only compare, not generate, so writers will forget and the file will lag; (3) the week-8 writer needs the weekly cast notes in the PR description anyway. Revisit when a cross-week contradiction is found by a reviewer for the second time (the project rule: a mistake seen twice becomes a rule); then build it the other way round, generated from the lessons (`pnpm content:cast` prints each person's facts per week), so there is nothing to maintain by hand, and use the printout as the writers' checklist.

## Round 8, review follow-ups

- **minimal-pair-accents accepted limit:** a US r-coloured vowel counts as ONE sound, so US `card`-`cad`, `part`-`pat`, `heart`-`hat` pass as minimal pairs in the US IPA (they are minimal in the UK only through a different vowel, /kɑːd/-/kæd/). Without that merge `work`-`walk` and `bird`-`bed` (a true single substitution in the US) would be false alarms; the cost is that an "r only" pair is not caught in the US.
- **fronted-variant meaning guards** (choice made, since the two readings cannot be told apart reliably): an adverb after `not`/`n't` gets no suggestion; an `after`/`before` phrase gets none when the sentence has a verb whose object the phrase may modify (like, love, hate, want, need, have, see, know, prefer, enjoy, be). After an activity verb with an object (`plays football after school`, `play a game after dinner`) the phrase is still demanded, because that is the week-7 incident. Remaining false positive: `I buy the bread after school` style sentences where the phrase does modify the noun. A capitalised first word keeps its capital unless it is a function word or a bank word (`London` stays; a rare common noun missing from the bank would keep a wrong capital in the suggestion).
- **cast-facts claim key** now includes the phrase after the object (`goes to school by bus` and `goes to school on foot` are two claims; `eats rice` and `eats rice for dinner` too), so a real contradiction hidden in such a pair (Dan `often` eats rice / `always` eats rice for dinner) is not found. A clause is cut at `but`/`and`; `Dan and Rose never cook` reads only the second person.
- **grammar-note header:** a bare dash cell (`-`, en or em dash) is the allowed way to write an empty corner cell and needs no Thai letter.

## Checkpoint 1 (day 30): weak-point attribution is per exercise

- A `minimal_pair` exercise carries one `phonemes` array for all its pairs, so a miss on any pair is attributed to every code of the exercise (`weakness:phoneme:*`). Checkpoint 1 limits the damage by grouping the 30 pairs into 10 exercises of 3-4 pairs with at most three closely related codes each (e.g. `["dh","d"]`, `["g","k"]`), but one miss still marks all codes of its exercise. The proper fix is a per-pair `pairs[].phonemes` field in the schema plus attribution in `derive.ts`; it is not done.
- `hot–heart` and `pot–part` (pron-03) differ by the vowel in UK English but only by the /r/ colouring in US English; `hut–hot` is the accent-robust pair of that exercise. The /ar/ vs /aa/ contrast cannot be a one-sound pair in both accents with taught words.
- Checkpoint accept lists contain forms the learner has not been taught (`years old`, `costs`): the validator only checks the first accepted translation against the taught words. They are there so that a correct answer typed by a learner who knows more is not marked wrong.
